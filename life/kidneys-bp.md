## Kidneys and Blood Pressure

I keep this explainer for the part that diet advice tends to skip: the kidneys are the body's primary long-term regulators of blood pressure. They set it over days and years by adjusting blood volume, releasing hormones, and nudging vessel tone, and their default job in a healthy body is to shed excess sodium and water.

### Volume control

The kidneys match sodium and water excretion to pressure, a loop known as pressure [natriuresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natriuresis). Pressure goes up, and they excrete more sodium and water, shrinking blood volume. Pressure drops, and they retain both, expanding it.

### The renin cascade

When kidney blood flow or sodium falls, the kidneys release renin, which starts the [renin–angiotensin system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renin%E2%80%93angiotensin_system): angiotensin II constricts vessels, aldosterone holds on to sodium and water, and vasopressin holds on to water. The net effect is higher pressure.

### Vessel signaling

The kidneys also make local signals that fine-tune vessel diameter. Prostaglandins and nitric oxide widen vessels; endothelin narrows them.

### What that means for diet

Sodium is the lever a person controls. Lowering [salt intake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_salt) lowers blood pressure in most people with hypertension, and that is the sourced advice behind the [blood pressure diet](bp-diet.md). For persistently [low blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypotension) the usual advice runs the other way: stay hydrated and avoid unnecessary sodium restriction.

### Why it matters

[Hypertensive kidney disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_kidney_disease) is the loop closing on itself: chronic kidney disease raises blood pressure, and uncontrolled high pressure damages the kidneys, so protecting the kidneys is part of controlling pressure for the long term.
