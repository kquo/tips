## The Problem with Infrastructure-as-Code

[Infrastructure-as-code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure_as_code) tools exist to compensate for something operating systems and clouds lack: a built-in, versioned record of their own configuration with a safe way back to any previous version. If Azure recorded every change and could roll back to any of them, most of what Terraform and Ansible do would be unnecessary. That simplifies, and the objection is fair: these tools also standardize, scaffold, orchestrate, track cost, and enforce compliance. But a built-in system could offer all of that too, and everyone would gain more if it did so through an open standard rather than each vendor's private mechanism.

It reminds me of early networking, when every vendor ran its own protocol and interfacing systems was a headache, until [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_protocol_suite) won. The world needs the equivalent for infrastructure: one universal management protocol built into every OS, cloud, and major service, giving interoperability across hybrid and multi-cloud setups, less complexity than a separate toolchain, and room for tool builders to move up the stack. The obstacles are real, since standardization needs cooperation among competitors and has to leave room for differentiation, and some players profit from the current mess. There are signs of movement: open-source tooling itself, the [Cloud Native Computing Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Native_Computing_Foundation) making Kubernetes a de facto standard, and the [IEEE intercloud interoperability standard](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/2302/7056/). We will see what the next few years bring.
