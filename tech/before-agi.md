## Software Before AGI

Most software still worth building before [artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence) arrives will, I think, be what I call a domain-specific harness (DSH): a specialized application that connects a model to one domain's tools, data, workflows, permissions, and users. If AGI becomes [superintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintelligence), even those harnesses stop being separately engineered. The system itself would create, modify, and operate whatever interfaces, integrations, controls, and security boundaries a task needs. Those layers would still exist functionally, but nobody would design and code them the way we write software today.

Until then, the utilities I want to write are small and very maintainable, augment both people and models, and help us reach a responsible, [aligned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_alignment) AGI sooner rather than later.

The division of labor I am aiming for is human intuition steering the exploration, with the AI, and soon the AGI, reasoning through the data, writing the analysis, coding, and designing the experiments.

Everything here carries a sell-by date. These claims describe what models could do when I wrote them, and capability has been moving faster than my notes during the current [AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom). Treat each one as provisional: if AGI arrives, and more so if it becomes superintelligence, most of this becomes obsolete, and even if it never does, the specifics will still age quickly.

Current as of September 2026.
