## Pseudoscience (Plain English)

[Pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience) looks like [science](science.md) but is not. It lacks evidence, method, and testing. It has no shared set of practices open to criticism and improvement. Believing it leads to bad decisions and harmful actions. Spreading it damages health, safety, and well-being. Wikipedia keeps a fine [list of topics characterized as pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_topics_characterized_as_pseudoscience). Pseudoscience is bad, and I say so plainly.

Some people are more drawn to it than others. In my experience they show little doubt about their own claims. They find it hard to rule out more likely explanations. They stop before a thought reaches its logical end. They are drawn to beliefs that feel good, bring comfort and predictability, and become part of who they are. When science offers a different view, the belief wins.

The cure is education and critical thinking: scientific literacy and a culture that values asking questions. But changing one believer's mind is so unlikely that most of the time it is not worth the effort. When you do engage, respect and rapport matter more than facts. The how is in the [Discourse Protocol](../society/discourse.md). Alone, you are up against the huge resources of an engagement business that profits from false information. That is why I favor pressing governments to regulate it within the First Amendment rather than ban it. Regulation, not ridicule, is the answer that scales.
